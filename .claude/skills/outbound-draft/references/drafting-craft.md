# Drafting craft — how an abstract line becomes one that lands

Read this when a beat is true but limp. Source: Harry Dry.

_(Written for the funnel-audit era, where the thing being sharpened was a
finding. Every rule below is about turning abstraction into something a reader
can see, so they apply unchanged to a hook, an identity beat or a close.)_

## The three rules (also the core of the gate)

**1. Can I visualize it?** If you can't picture it, she won't remember it. "every parent who downloads your guide just disappears" — you can see that. "there's a gap in your capture flow" — you can't see anything.

**2. Can I falsify it?** Provably true or false makes her sit up. "you've got 49K followers and none of them on your list" — checkable, sharp. "your audience isn't fully optimized" — nobody can argue, so nobody cares.

**3. Can nobody else say it?** Could another freelancer send this exact email to another coach with the name swapped? If yes, it's a template and it's dead. The hook must be bespoke to her.

## Make it concrete (zoom in)

Take the vague word and rewrite until you hit something you can see.
- Abstract: "no opt-in capture"
- Zoom: people download and leave
- Zoom: parents download her guide and vanish
- Concrete: "49K people go through your flow and you keep none of them"

Never send the abstract version.

## Make it falsifiable (point, don't talk)

You can't talk, you can only point. No adjectives doing the work — no "amazing," "powerful," "incredible." Point at the exact thing she said, on the exact date.
- Talking: "your checkout has issues"
- Pointing: "the flow ends on a Google Drive link, no email, no name, nothing"

A falsifiable line is you putting your head on the chopping block. That is what makes her read it.

## Make it bespoke (look deeper)

If the line could describe 500 coaches, you haven't looked hard enough. If you could paste it into another coach's email, rewrite it until you can't.

## Conflict — put it in every email

We judge relatively. Show the problem, then the gap, and both sharpen. Write it with a hidden "but": the thing was going one way, but. "you've got 40K people clicking your bio, they're growing everyone's list except yours." Don't open with "your funnel's good." Show what everyone does, then what she's missing.

## Facts — if in doubt, give a fact

A fact guarantees you've said something. Most outreach is word-shaped air. Never "I help coaches get more clients." Always the specific number, the specific person, the specific thing. "9 meetings in 6 weeks, 6 of them signed." Point at the graph, don't describe it.

## Small numbers, bigger timeframe

A coach losing a handful of parents a week is losing hundreds a year. The big number lands; the daily one doesn't.

## The quality bar

- 9/10 or it doesn't go.
- The One Mississippi test: does the line land in two seconds? Run subject lines through this hardest.
- The garden-walk test: done when you walk away, come back, and can't add or remove a word.
