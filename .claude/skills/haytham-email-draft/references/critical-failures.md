# Critical failures — learned from repeated user corrections

These are the most common ways a draft that "passes" the gate on a shallow
read is still wrong. Each has been corrected before, sometimes more than
once. Internalize them before every draft — this is what `references/gate.md`
line "Not sanded to a robotic shine" and "peer register" are checking for
concretely.

## Three-beat parallel structures (banned)

The kill list bans "not just X, but Y, and Z." The rule extends to ANY
three-item parallel structure inside one sentence — three clauses, three
verbs, or three negatives in series, not just that exact phrasing.

**Violations (illustrative):**
- "The cohort is well built, the voice is consistent, and the niche has weight." — three clauses in one sentence.
- "11,500 people visit, leave, and never hear from you again." — three verbs in series.
- "They are not in your inbox, not on your list, and not reachable." — three parallel negatives.

**Fix:** break it into separate sentences. "The cohort is well built. The
voice is consistent. The niche has weight." Each period is a pause for the
reader. Multiple short sentences in a row is NOT a three-beat — a beat
requires parallel clauses stacked inside one sentence, not several short
sentences back to back.

## Formulaic transitions (banned)

These read as templates the moment a reader has seen a cold email before:

- "The thing that got me though" / "What I couldn't shake" / "The thing I noticed" — overused discovery frames.
- A templated pivot question repeated across emails (e.g. always closing on "deliberate, or one of the things that hasn't been built yet?") — the SMYKM close is a real move once, a tell the second time it's reused verbatim.
- "I noticed..." / "I wanted to reach out..." — already on the kill list, but watch for paraphrases that dodge the exact words while keeping the same discovery-frame shape.
- "Anyway, the reason I'm writing..." — mechanics.md lists this as SMYKM opening A, a legitimate transition line. It is an option, not a default. Don't reach for it on every draft.

**Fix:** state the finding flatly. "Your site doesn't have an email
signup." No discovery frame, no "I went through your site and found..."
Just the fact.

## Math breakdowns that read like an audit

"Of the 11,500 followers who saw that post, 75 liked it and a few dozen
will comment MOTHER. The other 11,000 are not in your inbox..." — this
reads like a funnel report, not a human noticing something. It's also an
operator-vocabulary violation in disguise even when no banned word appears,
because the shape of a breakdown IS the audit.

**Fix:** the number is backdrop, not the argument. "11,500 followers and
nowhere for them to leave an email. They leave. Next launch, clean slate."
One number, one cost, done. This is the same instinct as drafting-craft.md's
"small numbers, bigger timeframe" rule, applied to how many numbers appear
in one place, not just which numbers.

## Subject-body disconnect

The SMYKM subject has to be echoed in the first body sentence. If the
subject is built around a specific hook (her CTA, a framework name, an
episode title) and the first body sentence jumps straight to the finding
without touching the hook, the subject reads disconnected from the email it
sits on top of — she opened for one thing and landed on another.

**Fix:** SMYKM opening A in mechanics.md — elaborate the hook first, then
bridge to the finding. First sentence: elaborate on the hook (this IS the
admiration — her CTA works, her framework holds up). Second sentence:
bridge to the finding. This is also how "admire first" and "state the
finding flat" stop being in tension — the hook elaboration is the
admiration, so the bridge sentence is free to state the finding directly
without a separate warm-up.

## The email OS vs. voice tension, resolved for cold Touch 1

Mechanics.md says line 1 is the finding, stated flat, no warm-up. Voice.md
says admire first. Both are true, and here is how they resolve on an actual
cold Touch 1 rather than staying an abstract contradiction to re-derive
every draft:

- The SMYKM subject IS the hook.
- First body sentence: elaborate on the hook — this is the admiration.
- Second sentence: bridge to the finding, stated flat, no separate
  discovery frame.

Illustrative: "Your comment MOTHER posts work for the parents who act that
morning. The rest of the 11,500 visit your site though. Nothing to sign up
for." Sentence one is the hook elaboration (admiration). Sentence two is
the finding, flat, no "I noticed."

## Motivation must feel real

Voice.md models stating the real motivation out loud: "I couldn't just sit
around and watch your work go underappreciated." Say why you're writing,
but keep it to one short line — the sentence right before it should have
already supplied the "it" the motivation line refers to, so it doesn't need
re-explaining. "I couldn't just watch it." is a complete, load-bearing
sentence when the prior sentence already put "people disappearing" in the
reader's head. Spelling out what "it" means again is padding — the burrito
test should catch that on its own, but it's a specific enough mistake to
name here.

## The close is an open door, not a bow

No "let me know." No "looking forward." No "if you're open to it." A
conditional offer with a concrete, visualizable picture ends the email
instead:

- Good: "If you want, I can show you what I'd put between the feed and a list."
- Bad: "Let me know if you'd like to chat about this."
- Bad: "If you're open to it, I'd love to show you some ideas."

The concrete picture (a specific thing she can see herself looking at) does
the work. The question mark at the end does not have to carry the weight
alone — a vague close with a question mark is still a bow, not a door.
