## UAE 1 — cold opener, WORK hook, single finding (Aliya Rajah, career/leadership, 8.7K, Inbox 1)

The finding: her homepage "Work with Me" section shows three boxes — 1:1 Coaching, Live Workshops, Corporate Training — but the "Live Workshops" box links to a page that returns a hard 404. A real, currently-advertised offer on her own entry page dead-ends.
SMYKM hook (WORK): her LinkedIn post from a week earlier on confidence being built through reflection, not just action — "allow time for nothingness and for your mind to be bored."

>
> You wrote about protecting real downtime, letting your mind actually sit idle instead of staying busy on purpose. Most confidence advice skips straight to tactics.
>
> Then I went to your site and clicked into Live Workshops from your homepage. It goes nowhere, a dead page.
>
> Anyone who reads that post and wants more than a one on one call hits the same wall.
>
> Is that link waiting on a new workshop page, or did group sessions get shelved for now?
>
> Haytham

Why it works: same opener shape as the parenting track — elaborate the WORK hook in her own terms (SMYKM opening A), bridge to one exact finding on one exact page, make the cost visual (hits the same wall), ask one real question, sign Haytham. **No price anywhere in email 1** — AED only enters the thread once there's a number to quote (see uae-track.md). The hook is hers alone; swap the name and it collapses.

## UAE 2 — cold opener + cold Touch-2 that carries a *new* finding (Carol Glynn, business/finance, national-media coach, Inbox 1)

The finding (bank #1): the same "Financial Empowerment" course is sold at two live prices at once — the Kajabi storefront homepage her own logo links to lists the tiers in dirhams (1,135 / 2,535 / 3,995 AED) while the /WeareLIVE sales page lists them in dollars ($358 / $998 / $1,850), the AED running up to ~40% under the USD at the fixed peg. A buyer who clicks her logo pays noticeably less than one who came through the sales page.
SMYKM hook (WORK): her stance that women who understand money change everything, and that after 25+ years auditing the world's largest investment institutions what people lack isn't knowledge, it's clarity.

**Touch 1 — Subject: "women who understand money"**

> Hey Carol
>

>

>
> So a buyer who clicks your logo pays noticeably less than one who came through the sales page. And two prices for one thing is exactly what makes a careful person pause right at the buy, which is the opposite of the clarity you sell.
>
> Is the dirham page just an old one that never came down, or is it meant to be live?
>
> Haytham

**Touch 2 (cold, day 3 — carries bank #2, a *different* finding) — Subject: "women who understand money" (same thread)**

> Hey Carol
>
> Another one from the same look around.
>
> Two of the boxes on your homepage, the personal finance coaching one and the workshops one, click through to a page that doesn't exist. The real pages are live, they're just sitting at a different link, so the pictures point at the wrong place.
>

>
> Is that something that shifted when the site was rebuilt?
>
> Haytham

Why it works: this is the UAE follow-up payload rule made concrete. Touch 2 does **not** bump Touch 1's price-mismatch finding — it spends the next unused entry in her Findings Bank (the dead service-tile links), named as its own felt cost with the fix left vague, and asks a fresh single question. "Another one from the same look around" is the whole transition. Every cold touch on this track carries something new (uae-track.md); a bare "just following up" is a wasted send. Contrast this directly with the retired Heba shape at the bottom of the file — that one bumped the *same* finding via time passing, which no longer qualifies as a payload here.

## UAE 3 — full warm arc: opener → turn-two → Loom → the price-discovery email (Donna Brown, leadership/life, Inbox 2)

The freshest full UAE arc in the file, and the only place the **price-discovery email** appears in a real thread. She has no usable public hook (channels dormant since 2023), so the opener runs SMYKM opening B — straight onto the finding.

The finding (bank #1): her "Your Core Blueprint" Stripe checkout offers "The Complete Growth Bundle" (AED 1,997) as an order-bump add-on — but the bundle already contains Your Core Blueprint, so anyone who adds it pays for the foundation course twice, at the moment of highest intent.

**Touch 1 (cold, opens on the finding — no hook) — Subject: "your core blueprint bundle"**

> Hey Donna
>
> Went through your site, the course line is genuinely well built. Real checkout, real pricing.
>
> One thing on the Core Blueprint checkout though. Right where someone's about to pay 597 AED, there's an add on for the Complete Growth Bundle at 1,997 AED. But that bundle already has the course inside it.
>
> So a buyer who adds it on pays for the same thing twice, at the exact moment she's handing over her card.
>
> Is that meant to be there, or did it get added as a cross sell before anyone caught the overlap?
>
> Haytham

**She replied** (~10:01 Dubai) — thanked him, clarified the bundle already includes the course, said she'd revisit the checkout to reduce the confusion. Sequence flips to Warm.

**Touch 2 (turn-two, warm — second finding + the Loom offer) — Subject: "Re: your core blueprint bundle"**

> Hey Donna
>
> Good to know, thanks for checking so fast.
>
> One more thing while I had the page open. The bundle badge says Save 35%, comparing 2,797 AED to 1,997 AED. The real difference works out closer to 29%. Small gap, but it's the kind of number someone doing the math before they buy will catch.
>
> I do the small stuff on the back end for coaches like you, the pages and pricing details that quietly chip at trust while you're focused on the actual coaching. Want me to record a quick walkthrough of what I'd tighten up?
>
> Haytham

**She replied** — happy to see it, and asked how he'd come across her page.

**Touch 3 (warm, Loom delivery + the price-discovery question) — Subject: "Re: your core blueprint bundle"**

> Hey Donna
>
> To answer your question, I was looking around at coaches in Dubai and yours was one of the few with a real checkout instead of just a contact form, so I stopped to look properly.
>
> Here it is: [Loom link]
>
> It covers the two from my emails, plus one more I found while I was in there.
>
> Once you've watched it, I'm curious, if someone took the whole site top to bottom, everything sorted and off your plate, what would you expect that to run?
>
> Haytham

Why it works, stage by stage:
- **The opener runs on the finding alone (opening B).** No hook existed, so there's no forced hook — the admiration is real ("genuinely well built") and the finding is exact and dated to the moment of payment. A missing hook is a resolved state, not a reason to stall.
- **The turn-two answers her honestly, then carries a second finding + the Loom offer.** It reveals what he does ("the small stuff on the back end … the pages and pricing details," never the word "funnel"), names a new finding as the reason, and offers the walkthrough as easier-to-show, not a pitch.
- **Touch 3 is the price-discovery email in the wild.** It delivers the Loom, then asks the discovery question — "if someone took the whole site top to bottom … what would you expect that to run?" — with **no number of his own anywhere in the email**. That question is the entire point of the UAE track: get her anchor before any price is on the table, logged verbatim with a Discovery Anchor. Note the scope ("the whole site top to bottom") is wider than the Track A fixes, so her answer reads against that framing. The canonical phrasings for this email live in `uae-track.md`; this is one of them adapted to the thread, never pasted.

## The throughline

Every one of these, both tracks: admired first and meant it, pointed at an exact thing on an exact page, made the cost visual, asked one real question, signed Haytham, left an open door. None could be sent to a different coach with the name swapped. That last test is the one that matters most. On the UAE track, add two: no price in the opener, and every cold follow-up carries something new.

## P1 — Lane 1 ambiguous leak, SMYKM hook, warm reply (Amanda, conscious parenting, 1.4K)

The finding: no email capture anywhere on site or Linktree. Only entry point is a Calendly call. Ambiguous — her slow/sacred brand positioning might mean this is intentional. Framed as a genuine question, not a diagnosed leak.
SMYKM hook: an original poem she posted on IG, "A Mother's Heart." Not a carousel, not a graphic. She wrote a poem. Specific, bespoke, proves real time in her content.

**Touch 1 — Subject: "a mother's heart"**

> Hey Amanda
>
> your mother's heart poem got me. the part about stitching it back together and having no choice but to keep going. I read it twice.
>
> then I went looking around your site, and noticed there's no newsletter or anything to opt into, just the call. and I couldn't tell if that's on purpose. your whole thing is slow and sacred, so maybe a list would feel off, maybe it's the opposite of what you're going for.
>
> but it also means someone who reads that poem and feels it has nowhere to go unless they're ready to book right then. and a lot of people aren't, even when they want to be.
>
> so I'm curious more than anything, is the no-list thing a choice, or just not something you've set up yet?
>
> Haytham

**Her reply:** thanked him warmly, confirmed the no-list is fully intentional — she runs her business soft and slow, no funnels, no optimization. Instagram is where she shows up. If someone isn't ready, she trusts they'll come back.

**Touch 2 — Subject: "a mother's heart" (same thread)**

> Hey Amanda
>
> that makes complete sense. the way you've built it fits the work, and honestly there's something right about that.
>
> appreciate you taking the time to explain it properly. wishing you and the people you work with well.
>
> Haytham

Why it works: the SMYKM hook is as bespoke as it gets — a specific original poem, not a tip or a reel. The finding is held with genuine uncertainty ("I couldn't tell if that's on purpose") rather than diagnosed as broken. That uncertainty IS the email — it earns trust because it doesn't assume. The cost is framed from the reader's perspective ("has nowhere to go unless they're ready to book right then"), not from a conversion angle. Touch 2 closes with warmth and no ask, the right call when the reply confirmed a philosophy not a problem to solve.

Note on ambiguous leaks: when the finding might be intentional, the email respects the ambiguity rather than asserting the diagnosis. "I'm curious more than anything" is a real question, not a soft pitch. That framing is what got the warm reply. This lead went Lost/Wrong fit, but the thread is a clean example of help-before-sell at its purest — the email did right by her even though there was nothing to close.

## What NOT to do — a documented mistake (Darlynn, podcast host, 1.7K IG / 26K podcast)

Lane 2, no structural leak. The finding: 26K podcast listeners, a real trust asset, but the free toolkit's last page sends listeners back to more free content with no bridge into any paid program.

**Touch 1 — Subject: "26k vs 1700" — Sent**

> Hey Darlynn
>
> 26K people listen to your podcast. 1,700 follow you on instagram.
>
> So the podcast is the real thing. That's where people actually trust you.
>
> But I went through your stop yelling toolkit and the last page sends them back to, the podcast. More free episodes. A mama goes free guide, free podcast, free blog, and that's the whole journey. Nowhere does she bump into the programs you actually run.
>
> You built the trust. The part that turns it into something is just missing.
>
> What takes a listener from the podcast to actually working with you, anything?
>
> Haytham

**Her reply, in full:** "Rude." She read the 26k vs 1700 framing as condescending toward her smaller Instagram following.

What went wrong: the underlying point (the podcast is the deeper trust asset) was true and could have read as a compliment. But naming both numbers in the same opening lines, before any rapport, meant the first thing she read was a comparison putting her smaller platform on display. The subject line repeated the contrast before the email even opened.

The rule this produced: when a hook or opener involves contrasting two metrics the lead owns, lead with the strength only. Don't put the smaller number in the same sentence or subject line, even to make the bigger number look better by comparison. No reply was sent to "Rude" — correctly. Once a lead reads an opener as rude, there's no follow-up that doesn't either over-apologize or escalate. Silence was the right move.

## Cold follow-up shape — the parenting-era version, and why the UAE track supersedes it (Adrienne vs. Heba)

Both parenting threads, included because the *shape* of the follow-up is the lesson. **Read this against the UAE rule, which now overrides it for the active track:** on the UAE track every cold follow-up must carry a NEW payload — the next unused banked finding, the Loom offer, or a disambiguating question (`uae-track.md`, and see Carol above). The Heba "bump the same finding via time passing" shape below no longer qualifies as a payload here; it is kept as parenting-era history and as the contrast that makes the UAE rule concrete.

**Adrienne Bishop (70K IG, Lane 1) — the mistake, three separate free findings across three touches:**

Touch 1 flagged one real finding: her podcast page loads completely empty, and she actively sends traffic there. Good, single, verifiable. Touch 2 introduced a second, unrelated finding (a broken merge field in her welcome email). Touch 3 introduced a third (no visible price on the coaching page) and closed by openly handing her a menu of three things to fix herself.

By touch 3, a reader in her position has received a full mini-audit for nothing across three emails and zero reasons left to reply — everything found so far is small enough to fix in the time it takes to read the email. This is the gratitude-trap shape spread across a cold sequence.

**Heba Kalakeche (24.9K IG, Lane 1) — the parenting-era "tighter" version:**

Touch 1 flagged one finding: placeholder testimonial cards on her live homepage before a webinar launch. Touch 2 bumped the same finding using time passing as the new angle ("the webinar's come and gone but the placeholder cards are probably still sitting there"). Touch 3 bumped it again, shorter, as a natural question. No new findings, still under the parenting free-value cap.

**The rule this produced (parenting), and where it now stops:** a parenting cold follow-up's job was to stay present without spending anything new, so bumping the *same* finding through what's changed was the correct, disciplined move. **On the UAE track this is no longer enough** — a same-finding time-passing bump reads as a bare bump and a spam signal, and the send gate (`crm-gate send --carries`) enforces a real new payload on Touch 2/3 (checked against the row's Findings Bank). If touch 2 or 3 would only exist by restating touch 1, on the UAE track that is a sign to either spend the next banked finding (Carol UAE 2), offer the Loom, or ask the disambiguating question — never to bump.
