## Example 1 — Lane 1 felt leak, SMYKM hook (Ghadir, educator, 54.8K)

The finding: bio -> Linktree -> single intake form asking full name, birth date, home address, signature before they've spoken to her. No freebie, no email capture. Friction filtering parents out before she hears from them.
SMYKM hook: her birthday post, "hello 35, a new chapter," two-cakes photo.

**Touch 1 — Subject: hello 35**

> Hey Ghadir
>
> two cakes for 35, not bad.
>
> you said new chapter, so I went and looked at where parents actually land when they try to reach you. one link, straight to a form asking for full name, birth date, home address, and a signature.
>
> before they've even spoken to you.
>
> 54K parents trust you enough to click. most of them close that tab before they finish.
>
> do you know how many start it and don't complete it?
>
> Haytham

**Touch 2 — Subject: "hello 35" (same thread)**

> Hey Ghadir
>
> the other side of it: the parents who close the tab, you have no way to reach them. they just disappear and you never knew they were there.
>
> still worth a look?
>
> Haytham

Why it works: the hook is hers alone. The finding is pointed (exact fields, exact path), not described. The cost is visual (close the tab, disappear). One question. Touch 2 adds a new angle (the unreachable parents), it doesn't just bump.

## Example 3 — Lane 1 felt leak, warm reply + turn-two (Pam, conscious parenting + mom mental health)

The finding: full sales page for her 6-month program, real testimonials, the works. At the bottom: "next kick-off call is June 1," three weeks stale. A mom who reads the whole thing and gets sold hits a passed date and feels she missed it.
SMYKM hook: she bet her whole thing on the body (somatic) instead of scripts, the way most parenting coaches sell.

**Touch 1 — Subject: "the damn good mama page"**

> Hey Pam
>
> most parenting coaches sell scripts, say this when they melt down, do that in the moment. you bet your whole thing on the body instead and committed to it hard enough to build a book, 400 episodes and a six month program around it. that's a real point of view, not many people have one.
>
> which is why this is worth flagging. I read the whole damn good mama page, got to the bottom ready to see what's next, and it says the kick off call is june 1. that was three weeks ago.
>
> so a mom who reads the entire thing and gets sold hits a date that already passed. feels like she missed it, closes the tab.
>
> is that date just stuck or is the next round not set yet?
>
> Haytham

**Her reply:** thanked him, fixed the page, next kickoff July 6, asked "is the program something you're interested in pursuing?"

**Touch 2 (turn-two, warm) — Subject: "Re: the damn good mama page"**

> Hey Pam
>
> glad it was an easy fix.
>
> and to answer you straight, I'm not a parent in the market for the program. I do the behind the scenes side for coaches, funnels and launches, the parts that lose people quietly while you're focused on the actual work. a passed kickoff date on a live sales page is a classic one.
>
> you've clearly built something real, so I'm not going to pitch you. but if a second set of eyes on the back end before July 6 would help, happy to take a look.
>
> either way, glad the page is sorted.
>
> Haytham

Why it works: this one runs long and is better for it — the admiration is doing real work, so length lost to the voice on purpose. The hook names her point of view without quoting her content. The finding is concrete and dated. The turn-two reply is honest before it's anything else (clears up that he's not a buyer), stays peer, offers help without pushing, and leaves on an open door. This is help-before-sell in its purest form.

## The throughline

Every one of these: admired first and meant it, pointed at an exact thing on an exact page, made the cost visual, asked one real question, signed Haytham, left an open door. None could be sent to a different coach with the name swapped. That last test is the one that matters most.

## Example 4 — Lane 1 ambiguous leak, SMYKM hook, warm reply (Amanda, conscious parenting, 1.4K)

The finding: no email capture anywhere on site or Linktree. Only entry point is a Calendly call. Ambiguous — her slow/sacred brand positioning might mean this is intentional. Framed as a genuine question, not a diagnosed leak.
SMYKM hook: an original poem she posted on IG, "A Mother's Heart." Not a carousel, not a graphic. She wrote a poem. Specific, bespoke, proves real time in her content.

**Touch 1 — Subject: "a mother's heart"**

> Hey Amanda
>
> your mother's heart poem got me. the part about stitching it back together and having no choice but to keep going. I read it twice.
>
> then I went looking around your site, and noticed there's no newsletter or anything to opt into, just the call. and I couldn't tell if that's on purpose. your whole thing is slow and sacred, so maybe a list would feel off, maybe it's the opposite of what you're going for.
>
> but it also means someone who reads that poem and feels it has nowhere to go unless they're ready to book right then. and a lot of people aren't, even when they want to be.
>
> so I'm curious more than anything, is the no-list thing a choice, or just not something you've set up yet?
>
> Haytham

**Her reply:** thanked him warmly, confirmed the no-list is fully intentional — she runs her business soft and slow, no funnels, no optimization. Instagram is where she shows up. If someone isn't ready, she trusts they'll come back.

**Touch 2 — Subject: "a mother's heart" (same thread)**

> Hey Amanda
>
> that makes complete sense. the way you've built it fits the work, and honestly there's something right about that.
>
> appreciate you taking the time to explain it properly. wishing you and the people you work with well.
>
> Haytham

Why it works: the SMYKM hook is as bespoke as it gets — a specific original poem, not a tip or a reel. The finding is held with genuine uncertainty ("I couldn't tell if that's on purpose") rather than diagnosed as broken. That uncertainty IS the email — it earns trust because it doesn't assume. The cost is framed from the reader's perspective ("has nowhere to go unless they're ready to book right then"), not from a conversion angle. Touch 2 closes with warmth and no ask, right call when the reply confirmed a philosophy not a problem to solve.

Note on ambiguous leaks: when the finding might be intentional, the email respects the ambiguity rather than asserting the diagnosis. "I'm curious more than anything" is a real question, not a soft pitch. That framing is what got the warm reply. This lead went Lost/Wrong fit, but the thread is a clean example of help-before-sell at its purest — the email did right by her even though there was nothing to close.

SMYKM hook: a recent IG post — her 21-year-old son texting a lemon emoji, code for "I want to come home, but I need you to be the reason." She's been telling his story since he was six.

Neither touch has replied as of this writing. Included because the sequence shows how a cold thread with zero engagement can still fold two additional held-back findings into a clean, current-priced close, without ever giving them away for free first.

- **The tone stays warm even after two silences.** "I get how something like this ends up sitting behind everything else on your plate" reads as understanding, not passive-aggressive, before the close.

## What NOT to do — a documented mistake (Darlynn, podcast host, 1.7K IG / 26K podcast)

Lane 2, no structural leak. The finding: 26K podcast listeners, a real and large trust asset, but the free toolkit's last page sends listeners back to more free content (the podcast, Instagram) with no bridge into any of her three paid programs at all.

**Touch 1 — Subject: "26k vs 1700" — Sent**

> Hey Darlynn
>
> 26K people listen to your podcast. 1,700 follow you on instagram.
>
> So the podcast is the real thing. That's where people actually trust you.
>
> But I went through your stop yelling toolkit and the last page sends them back to, the podcast. More free episodes. A mama goes free guide, free podcast, free blog, and that's the whole journey. Nowhere does she bump into the programs you actually run.
>
> You built the trust. The part that turns it into something is just missing.
>
> What takes a listener from the podcast to actually working with you, anything?
>
> Haytham

**Her reply, in full:** "Rude." She read the 26k vs 1700 framing as condescending toward her smaller Instagram following.

What went wrong: the underlying point (the podcast is the deeper trust asset, more than follower count) was true and could have read as a genuine compliment. But naming both numbers in the same opening lines, before any rapport existed, meant the first thing she read was a comparison putting her smaller platform on display. The subject line repeated the contrast before the email even opened. Sentence case fact-stacking ("26K... 1,700...") reads as scorekeeping when there's no established trust yet to carry the intended compliment.

The rule this produced: when a SMYKM hook or opener involves contrasting two metrics the lead owns, lead with the strength only. Don't put the smaller number in the same sentence or subject line, even to make the bigger number look better by comparison. If the gap itself needs naming as part of the finding, find a way to do it without restating both numbers back at her, especially not as the very first thing she reads.

This lead was closed Lost after one touch, Lane 2 (no felt leak forced into the open anyway, since the finding was real but the framing made it land as criticism rather than help). No reply was sent to the "Rude" message, correctly. Once a lead reads an opener as rude, there's no version of a follow-up that doesn't either over-apologize or escalate. Silence was the right move.

## Cold follow-up shape — what NOT to do vs. the tighter version (Adrienne vs. Heba, both no reply yet, Jun-Jul 2026)

Neither thread has replied as of Jul 2. Both are included because the shape of the follow-up itself is the lesson, independent of outcome — this is what a correctly-run cold sequence should look like once the free-value cap applies to follow-ups, not just money emails.

**Adrienne Bishop (70K IG, Lane 1) — the mistake, three separate free findings across three touches:**

Touch 1 flagged one real finding: her podcast page loads completely empty, and she actively sends traffic there from her bio, nav, and welcome email. Good, single, verifiable.

Touch 2 introduced a second, unrelated finding: her welcome email's merge field is broken ("Hello ," with a blank where the name should go). This is a new diagnosis, not a bump.

Touch 3 introduced a third: her coaching page has no visible price, just a free-consult CTA — and closed with "still think the podcast page is the one to fix first though," openly handing her a menu of three things to fix herself, none of which required Haytham once named.

By touch 3, a reader in her position has received a full mini-audit for nothing across three separate emails and zero reasons left to reply, because everything found so far is small enough to fix in the time it takes to read the email. This is the exact gratitude-trap shape, just spread across a cold sequence instead of a single warm thread.

**Heba Kalakeche (24.9K IG, Lane 1) — the tighter version:**

Touch 1 flagged one finding: three visible placeholder testimonial cards on her live homepage, four days before a webinar launch actively driving traffic to that same page.

Touch 2 bumped the same finding using time passing as the new angle: "the webinar's come and gone but the placeholder cards are probably still sitting on your homepage... every parent landing there right now sees dashed borders and italic filler text before they see anything about you." No new information, no second finding — the same fact restated through what's changed (the webinar happened) rather than what's new.

Touch 3 bumped it again, shorter, using a natural question instead of a restatement: "curious how the 7 spots went, whether they filled before or after people hit those placeholder cards on the way there... the next thing you launch will send people through that same homepage first." Still zero new findings, still under the cap, and it reframes the same fact as an ongoing cost rather than a one-time note.

**The rule this produced:** a cold follow-up's job is to stay present in the inbox without spending anything new. If touch 2 or 3 would only exist by finding something else wrong, that's a sign the site has more than one real issue — which is useful, but it's Loom material or "Next Three" bonus material once a price is on the table, not a second free gift in a thread that hasn't converted the first one.
