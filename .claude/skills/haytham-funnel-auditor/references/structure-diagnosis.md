# Structure Diagnosis — Domain 3

A multi-step funnel is a sequence of psychological transitions. Every page has one job. When a funnel leaks, diagnose where people drop and what psychological job the page was failing to do.

---

## Framework 1 — Customer Value Journey (Ryan Deiss, 8 Stages)

Funnels leak when a page tries to force a leap across stages, or when a page does the wrong stage's job.

**Stage 1 — Aware:** prospect discovers the brand.
- Failure pattern (Premature Pitch): driving cold traffic directly to a high-ticket sales letter or application page before any trust is established.

**Stage 2 — Engage:** deepen the relationship through valuable content.
- Failure pattern: distracting engaged readers with external social media links or blog posts that lead to dead ends.

**Stage 3 — Subscribe:** secure permission to contact by offering a lead magnet.
- Failure pattern: asking for excessive information (phone, revenue, team size) on the initial opt-in form, collapsing opt-in rates.

**Stage 4 — Convert:** turn subscribers into first-time buyers via a low-friction entry offer.
- Failure pattern (Orphan Lead): acquiring email leads but failing to present a clear, immediate opportunity to transact. Warm subscribers sit passively on the list with no defined pathway to buy.

**Stage 5 — Excite:** capitalize on the purchase dopamine with a fast onboarding win.
- Failure pattern (Excite Void): no immediate "speed-to-value" post-purchase. Buyer experiences immediate remorse because there's no early win delivered.

**Stage 6 — Ascend:** introduce core and premium offers to generate profitability.
- Failure pattern (Dead-End Ascension): client successfully completes the core program but the brand has no continuity or premium offer, capping lifetime value.

**Stage 7 — Advocate:** encourage reviews and testimonials.
- Failure pattern: passive reliance on customers volunteering reviews; failing to request at the moment of maximum satisfaction.

**Stage 8 — Promote:** turn advocates into active promoters.
- Failure pattern: complicated affiliate systems with no marketing assets or poor incentive alignment.

**Diagnostic question:** At which stage is the funnel breaking? Name it. The stage tells you the failure pattern.

## Framework 2 — One Job Per Page

The most common architecture error: a single page trying to accomplish multiple business objectives simultaneously.

If a landing page asks the reader to download a free report, watch a webinar, browse a features list, read blog posts, AND book a call — all on the same screen — decision paralysis sets in and they take no action.

**Visual CTA Audit:** count the distinct calls to action on the page. If there are navigation links in the header, external links in the footer, and multiple buttons driving different outcomes, the page is broken.

A high-converting page has one primary action. All secondary design elements and copy support that single outcome.

**Diagnostic question:** Does this page have one job, or is it trying to educate, compare, and close simultaneously?

## Framework 3 — Friction Audit (MECLABS Friction Heuristic)

Friction is the silent revenue killer. Two distinct dimensions:

**Length friction:** the absolute number of form fields, screens, or pages a prospect must navigate to complete the conversion. Every additional field lowers completion rate.

**Difficulty friction:** complexity of the task, confusing form labels, disruptive visual layouts, slow page load times, unclear next steps.

**Progressive profiling principle:** the volume of information requested must match the buyer's level of trust and intent.
- Cold traffic / first opt-in: email only. Nothing else.
- Deeper in the funnel, higher intent: progressively request more.

Asking for a phone number and annual revenue on the first opt-in form is asking for high-friction data before earning the right to it.

**Diagnostic question:** Are we requesting high-friction information before establishing basic value? Is friction calibrated to trust level?

## Framework 4 — Traffic Temperature Alignment

The same page cannot convert cold, warm, and hot traffic equally. Funnels fail when they serve the same experience to all three.

**Cold traffic (zero trust):**
Needs highly educational, proof-heavy page architecture. Goal: establish expertise, address symptoms, request low-risk micro-commitment (email or $20 tripwire). Cannot ask for high-ticket sale.

**Warm traffic (familiarity):**
Needs methodological verification copy. These users are Solution- or Product-Aware. They need case studies, deep-dives into the framework mechanics, answers to specific operational objections.

**Hot traffic (high intent):**
Needs a direct close architecture. These users are Most Aware. Don't re-educate them. Clean, zero-friction money page, pricing transparency, fast one-page checkout.

**Diagnostic question:** Is the page copy calibrated to the temperature of the incoming traffic source? Is cold traffic landing on a hot-traffic page?

## Framework 5 — Post-Purchase Flow (the most ignored revenue leak)

Most optimization stops at the moment of purchase. Three critical post-purchase elements routinely left broken:

**The thank-you page:**
A standard "thank you, check your email for access" page is wasted high-intent attention. At the moment of maximum purchase excitement, the buyer is most receptive. The thank-you page should: deliver an immediate, low-friction win, present an immediate secondary offer, or direct the buyer into a community to drive early retention.

**The welcome sequence:**
The welcome email flow must fight buyer's remorse. Not dry and informational — it should re-state the original value proposition, validate the buyer's decision, and guide them step-by-step through their first interaction with the product. Non-consumption is the primary driver of refund requests.

**The post-purchase consumption flow:**
For coaching and info-products, non-consumption is the primary churn driver. If a buyer stalls after module 1, an automated follow-up sequence should trigger to re-engage them with encouragement and a micro-win prompt.

**Diagnostic question:** Does the funnel optimize for what happens after the purchase, or does it treat the sale as the finish line?

## The Orphan Lead Pattern (specific to coaching funnels)

Common in parenting and faith-based coaching funnels: the coach has a strong free lead magnet, a good delivery mechanism, and an engaged email list — but no clear, immediate path from the free thing to the paid thing. Subscribers are warm but there's no bridge.

Symptoms: high freebie opt-in rate, engaged open rates, but near-zero conversion to paid. Coaches often interpret this as "my audience isn't ready to buy" when the real issue is that there's no obvious next step offered at the moment of maximum goodwill (right after the freebie delivers).

## Structure Diagnostic Matrix

| Structural node | Strategic question | Invisible failure mode | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Page-job purity | Does this page focus on a single action? | Low overall conversion; heatmaps show random clicks on header links, footer terms, social badges | Strip global nav from landing pages. One primary CTA button visible |
| Friction audit | Are we requesting high-friction info before establishing value? | High drop-off on opt-in forms; users click CTA but abandon when form appears | Minimize initial fields to email only. Move qualifying questions to step-two survey |
| Temperature match | Is page copy calibrated to incoming traffic temperature? | High ad spend, near-zero conversions on short-form pitch page targeting cold audiences | Restructure to lead with symptom education and social proof before pitching |
| Welcome flow | Does the welcome sequence validate the purchase and lower financial anxiety? | High refund rates within 14 days; buyers consume module 1 then disappear | Implement post-purchase email series celebrating early micro-wins, driving portal consumption |
| Thank-you page | Is the thank-you page a tactical transition or a dead end? | High list drop-off; prospects opt in but fail to open subsequent emails | Embed immediate low-friction OTO or high-intent video to capture peak attention |
| Orphan lead | After the freebie delivers, is there a clear immediate path to the paid offer? | High freebie downloads, engaged list, near-zero paid conversion | Add a direct, timed offer in the welcome sequence (within 24-48 hours of delivery) |
