# Offer Diagnosis — Domain 2

When a funnel underperforms, most people rewrite the copy. The root cause is often a structurally weak offer. Distinguishing between a copy failure and an offer failure is critical — the remedies are completely different.

---

## Copy Failure vs. Offer Failure — The Behavioral Tell

**Copy failure indicators:**
- Low average time on page
- Sharp scroll-depth drop-off in the first 25% of the page
- Minimal clicks to checkout or pricing
- Copy failed to hold attention or establish relevance

**Offer failure indicators:**
- High time on page (3+ minutes)
- Deep scroll depth (prospects reading the full page)
- Pricing/checkout page has high open rate
- But purchase conversion rate is very low
- The copy built interest and desire — but when presented with the actual transaction terms, the perceived value collapsed

Symptom: "they loved the content but didn't buy." That's almost always an offer problem, not a copy problem.

---

## Framework 1 — Hormozi's Value Equation (4 Diagnostic Levers)

```
                   Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
Perceived Value = ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                         Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice
```

Diagnose each lever separately. A weak numerator or a heavy denominator both kill perceived value.

**Lever 1 — Dream Outcome (numerator)**
Is the offer selling an intermediate skill or the ultimate transformation the buyer desires?
- Weak: "better communication tools"
- Strong: "stop the screaming, get your evenings back"
The offer must name the finish line, not the vehicle.

**Lever 2 — Perceived Likelihood of Achievement (numerator)**
Does the prospect believe they can actually make this work?
Failure tell: "this sounds great but I'm not sure I can do it." Symptom: high engagement, low purchase, feedback showing self-doubt rather than price objection.
Fix: specific case studies of similar people, clear step-by-step methodology, done-for-you components, risk reversal.

**Lever 3 — Time Delay (denominator)**
How long between payment and the first tangible win?
A 12-week program where the student sees no result until week 12 is vulnerable. The gap between purchase and first proof of progress is where buyer's remorse lives.
Fix: "quick-start" assets, an immediate audit, a template that delivers a win within 48 hours of purchase.

**Lever 4 — Effort & Sacrifice (denominator)**
How much active work does the buyer have to do?
A program requiring 60 hours of raw video and DIY software builds suffers high drop-off.
Fix: done-for-you templates, pre-written swipe files, integration scripts that minimize labor.

## Framework 2 — The Guarantee as a Diagnostic Lever

A guarantee is not just a trust badge. It directly affects two levers:
- **Increases** Perceived Likelihood of Achievement — transfers financial risk from buyer to seller
- **Decreases** Effort & Sacrifice perception — reassures that even if the process is hard, they won't lose money

A weak or absent guarantee ("no refunds under any circumstances") signals seller insecurity and immediately depresses perceived likelihood. It is a tell that the seller doesn't believe in their own result.

**Diagnostic question:** Does the guarantee clearly transfer the risk of failure from the buyer to the seller, or is it a hollow "satisfaction guaranteed" with no teeth?

## Framework 3 — Vitamin vs. Painkiller Diagnosis

**Vitamin offer (nice to have):**
- Proactive, preventative, long-term optimization
- "Learn productivity strategies," "improve your general wellness"
- The negative consequences of ignoring it are delayed
- Customer acquisition cost is high, sales cycles are long, pricing power is limited
- Buyer agrees it's "good" but declines to buy now

**Painkiller offer (immediate relief):**
- Addresses an immediate, acute, felt threat or crisis
- Buyer is actively searching for a solution
- High price insensitivity — they want it resolved now
- Short sales cycle

**The coaching niche trap:** most parenting and faith-based coaching programs are structured as vitamins (long-term transformation, gradual improvement) but sold as painkillers (urgent emotional language). The mismatch kills conversion because the framing creates urgency the offer doesn't actually deliver.

**How to diagnose:** Ask — what happens to this buyer if they don't buy today? If the honest answer is "nothing changes immediately," it's a vitamin. If the honest answer is "the problem gets actively worse," it's a painkiller.

**Pivot path (vitamin to painkiller):**
1. Isolate the acute pain point — the "3AM panic" — not the general improvement
2. Reformulate the value proposition around immediate crisis resolution, not gradual transformation
3. Introduce diagnostic audits or done-for-you components that solve the specific pain immediately, demonstrating early proof within days

## Framework 4 — Offer Ladder and Price Anchoring

**Price anchoring failure (presenting cheapest first):**
The first price seen becomes the anchor. If a $47 tripwire is presented before a $2,000 core offer, the $2,000 feels expensive. Reversed: show a $10,000 mastermind first, and a $2,000 core offer feels highly accessible.

**Diagnostic question:** Is the core offer priced without a high-ticket anchor, or are products sequenced from cheapest to most expensive?

**Disconnected value ladder:**
Every step of the ladder must solve the immediate problem created by the previous step, while simultaneously exposing the next logical challenge. If the $20 book solves a completely unrelated problem from the $5,000 mastermind, the buyer has no natural reason to ascend.

**Diagnostic question:** Does each rung of the value ladder solve a problem created by the previous rung?

**Order bump / OTO absence:**
The checkout page is the highest-intent moment in the entire funnel. A bare checkout with no order bump or one-time offer leaves significant revenue on the table. Real receipt: PWH $522 from one order bump on a Kajabi checkout.

**Diagnostic question:** Is the checkout page being used to capture additional value at the peak of buying intent?

## Offer Diagnostic Matrix

| Diagnostic focus | Strategic question | Invisible failure mode | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copy vs. offer | Is checkout bounce rate high despite long session duration on the sales page? | Prospects read everything, proceed to checkout, then abandon at payment terms | Run price elasticity testing. Reframe payment terms or restructure bonus stack |
| Dream outcome | Is the offer positioned as a process or a specific desired transformation? | Long sales cycles; interest without a defined finish line | Rewrite core offer statement: [result] + [timeframe] + [objection handle] |
| Perceived likelihood | Does the prospect believe they can execute the framework? | High engagement, qualitative feedback shows self-doubt | Add done-for-you templates or risk reversal guarantee addressing execution risk |
| Vitamin vs. painkiller | Is the offer solving an immediate felt threat or a long-term nice-to-have? | Low purchase intent, high price sensitivity; "sounds good but not right now" | Recalibrate to target immediate threats, shorten time to first value |
| Price anchoring | Is the core offer shown without a premium anchor? | Mid-tier price feels expensive without context | Present ultra-premium offer first to anchor, making core feel accessible |
