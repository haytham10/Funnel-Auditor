# Copy Diagnosis — Domain 1

Copywriting failure is almost always a failure of clarity and psychological alignment, not insufficient persuasion power. Diagnosing it means running structured tests, not reading vibes.

---

## Framework 1 — Schwartz Awareness Levels

Five stages: Unaware, Problem-Aware, Solution-Aware, Product-Aware, Most Aware.

The primary diagnostic hypothesis when a page fails to convert: the page's copy entry point doesn't match the prospect's starting awareness level.

**Stage-Mixing (the most common invisible failure):** a single page simultaneously educates an unaware prospect, compares the product to competitors, and attempts a hard close. The reader is forced to process conflicting emotional and logical stages at once. Symptom: high bounce rate from top-of-funnel traffic even though the copy "looks good."

**Traffic temperature tells:**
- Cold traffic (unaware/problem-aware): needs symptoms, stories, education first. Sending cold traffic to a direct pitch page is the Premature Pitch failure.
- Warm traffic (solution/product-aware): needs case studies, framework mechanics, objection handling.
- Hot traffic (most aware): needs a clean, frictionless money page. Don't re-educate someone who's ready to buy.

**Diagnostic question:** Is the page speaking to the buyer's starting stage of awareness, or does it assume product familiarity too early?

---

## Framework 2 — MECLABS Conversion Heuristic

Conversion probability = (motivation × value clarity) + incentive - friction - anxiety

The key insight: motivation is largely external (you can't change it on the page). Value clarity is what you control. When a page underperforms, the first diagnostic lever is whether the value proposition is clear enough — not whether it's persuasive enough.

**Net force test:** Gross force of value (appeal + exclusivity) must outweigh gross force of cost (material + mental). Underperforming copy routinely fails because the mental cost of decoding the message is too high, and credibility is undermined by unquantified claims.

**Diagnostic question:** Can a cold visitor accurately explain exactly what is being sold within five seconds of landing?

---

## Framework 3 — The 5 Specific Tells of Weak Copy

**1. Feature-led instead of outcome-led**
Copy focuses on delivery mechanics ("12 weekly Zoom modules," "240-page workbook") rather than the transformation or relief-moment the buyer wants. The "So What" test catches this: read every benefit claim and ask "so what?" If it lands on a mechanism rather than a visceral outcome, it's feature-led.

**2. No single big idea or promise**
The page tries to solve multiple unrelated problems, diluting focus. High-converting pages: one reader, one big idea, one core promise, one CTA. Multiple CTAs or competing offers = decision paralysis = no action.

**3. Kaplan's Law violations (jargon and adjectives)**
"Any word not working for you is working against you." Filler words like "supercharge," "empower," "world-class," "cutting-edge" are abstract, unverifiable, and trigger cynicism in sophisticated buyers. Every adjective should be replaced with a fact or cut.

**4. Buried value proposition**
The core promise is obscured beneath a welcome message or a long founder story. The primary promise must be visible above the fold within the first 5 seconds. Symptom: hot traffic bounces fast — they arrived ready to buy but couldn't find the offer.

**5. Broken emotional and logical throughline**
The page doesn't guide the reader down a cohesive path. Every line's only job is to pull the reader to the next line (the slippery slide). Symptom: high drop-off at page transitions, users consume the hero but stop reading the programmatic details below.

---

## Framework 4 — Headline and Lead Assessment

The headline and lead (first 100-300 words) are responsible for up to 80% of page conversion performance. Diagnose the lead with AIDA: Attention (headline), Interest, Desire, Action.

**Message-match failure:** if the ad or email that drove traffic promised one thing and the headline says another, trust is destroyed instantly. The diagnostic: what did the traffic source promise? Does the page headline match it exactly?

**The relief-moment:** the lead must identify a specific, visceral fear (the "3AM panic") and pair it with honest, direct information and a clear next step. Boring, passive, or overly academic leads lose the reader before they reach the offer.

---

## Framework 5 — Proof, Specificity, Falsifiability (Harry Dry's 3 Rules)

Every claim must pass three tests:
1. **Can I visualize it?** If you can't picture it, it won't be remembered.
2. **Can I falsify it?** Is it provably true or false? Unfalsifiable superlatives ("industry-leading," "ultimate success protocol") are ignored by sophisticated buyers.
3. **Can nobody else say it?** Could a competitor sign this line? If yes, it's a generic claim.

**Falsifiability in practice:** "Our program helped 90% of participants increase revenue by 15%+ within 90 days, verified in our 2025 impact report" is falsifiable and builds trust. "Our coaching offers the ultimate success protocol" is not.

Replace subjective adjectives with: concrete facts, timestamped proof, specific figures.

---

## The 7 Copy Sweeps (Copyhackers — run sequentially)

When diagnosing existing copy before recommending rewrites, run these in order:

1. **Clarity sweep:** can the offer be understood within 5 seconds?
2. **Voice and tone sweep:** consistent, on-brand register throughout?
3. **So What sweep:** every claim lands on a visceral outcome, not a mechanism?
4. **Prove It sweep:** every promise backed by specific, verifiable proof?
5. **Specificity sweep:** vague filler words replaced with precise numbers and outcomes?
6. **Heightened Emotion sweep:** sensory-rich language aligned to the reader's actual frustrations and desires?
7. **Zero Risk sweep:** risk fully reversed near CTAs and pricing via strong, explicit guarantees?

## Copy Diagnostic Matrix

| What to evaluate | Diagnostic question | Invisible failure mode | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Awareness calibration | Is the page speaking to the buyer's starting awareness stage? | High bounce from top-of-funnel traffic; users engage with symptoms but exit at the pitch | Segment traffic temperature. Match problem-focused pages to cold traffic |
| Clarity vs. cleverness | Can a cold visitor explain the offer within 5 seconds? | Deep scroll, no CTA click — confusion about what's being sold | Simplify headline using voice-of-customer language. Remove metaphors |
| Falsifiability | Does the copy contain claims that can be objectively proven false? | Mid-page drop-off; readers express skepticism in feedback | Replace superlatives with quantified, verified data points |
| Value prop position | Is the primary promise above the fold? | Short session duration; hot traffic bounces in seconds | Move core promise to hero section. Strip nav to eliminate distraction |
| Emotional throughline | Does every sentence pull the reader to the next? | Drop-off at page transitions; hero consumed, details skipped | Run So What and Clarity sweeps. Cut non-essential sentences |
