What I do, and what I sell Dubai · July 2026

I'm Haytham.

I get coaches their next client.

A coach in Dubai has one problem that outranks all the others: where does the next client come from? Most of them post on Instagram and hope. I find the right people, I write to each of them personally, and I hand the coach booked calls with the ones who answer.

If you read nothing else
Who I sell to Solo coaches based in the UAE — business, leadership, life, health. Not agencies, not big firms. One person whose income depends on filling their own calendar.
What I give away Ten names. Ten real people who'd make a good client for that coach, already researched, handed over on a fifteen-minute call. Free, and no price is ever mentioned before it.
What I sell Five booked calls in thirty days, for $1,500. I do the whole thing — find the people, write the emails, send them from my own accounts, and turn the replies into appointments on the coach's calendar.
Why a coach says yes One new client is worth about $3,000 to them. So five calls only have to produce one for it to pay for itself twice over.
Why they trust me I prove it before I ask for anything. The ten free names are real, checkable in two minutes, and handed over before money comes up at all.
Where it stands The free half has been running for months. The paid half has never been sold to anyone. The last section on this page is the unflattering detail.
Step oneRunning now

The ten names — free

$0 this is the entire content of every email I send

A coach gets an email from me that mentions something specific and recent they actually did — a post they wrote, a podcast they went on. Then it offers one thing: ten people who fit the kind of client you want, already pulled, yours on a fifteen-minute call. I'll tell you why those ten and not the other forty.

That's all of it. No price, no pitch, no attachment. The email is under a hundred words and I sign it with my first name.

Why I give away the valuable part Because a promise costs nothing to make and nothing to ignore. A coach can check ten real names in two minutes, and once they have, I'm no longer a stranger selling something. The free thing is the door, not the product.
The obligation that creates for me The ten have to actually exist, on the call or within a day of it — not researched afterwards. It is the first thing the coach can check, and it comes due before I have sold anything. Breaking that promise would cost me more than never making it.

My guarantee

Five booked calls in thirty days, or I keep sending free until you have them. If you have zero after thirty days, the $1,500 comes back and you keep every name, every hook and every email account I built.

Two halves doing two different jobs. If the coach ends up with one, two, three or four calls, I keep working for free. Only zero triggers a refund. Splitting it that way means neither half has to carry the whole objection on its own.

Three conditions, and I say them up front

  • They reply to interested people within a day.
  • They keep a calendar link live with real availability on it.
  • They don't change who they're targeting halfway through. One change mid-sprint restarts the clock.

These aren't fine print protecting me. They're the three things that, if the coach doesn't do them, make the guarantee impossible for me to keep no matter how good the work is.

Why I don't just sell more of these

Six clients at a time. Two new starts a month. Sprints begin on the 1st and the 15th — miss one and you wait two weeks. That's a real limit, not a sales tactic: this work is hours, and I only have so many. I never discount to create urgency, because a discount that manufactures a deadline just teaches the buyer my price was soft.

Any business that grows without borrowing has to get paid faster than it spends. This one gets paid roughly nine times faster — which looks like the headline and isn't.

The honest reading Clearing that bar so easily tells me money was never the thing holding this back. Hours were. So the right move isn't to sell more of it cheaper. It's to hold the six-client limit and raise the price once the guarantee has been paid out on at least once and survived.

At full capacity with the three-month version taken up, that's roughly $15,000 a month against about $720 of costs — from six clients, not six hundred.

Sequence

The order I build it in

Not a timeline. Each step is unlocked by a fact becoming true, not by a date arriving.

  1. Sell The First Five three times at the full $1,500. Nothing else is on the table — no monthly, no discount, no cheaper version. Three sales is my dataset.
  2. Actually deliver five calls, once. This is the single gate. Until one sprint has produced five booked calls, my guarantee is a liability and everything after it has nothing behind it.
  3. Then add the cheaper options for people who say no. Both are deliverable today, and both stay off the table until the main thing has a track record.
  4. Then add the three-month version — once delivery is reliable, not merely possible.
  5. Then the ongoing monthly, last, and only to someone who has already been through a sprint with me.
  6. Then raise the price. $1,500 is a launch number, not a final one.
Straight answers

Where this honestly stands

Anyone reading this deserves the unflattering half, so here it is.

Nothing on the paid side has been sold

Every price above is reasoned from what a coach's client is worth to them. Not one of them has been validated by an actual transaction yet.

My guarantee has never been tested

What it costs me to honour is unknown until I pay out on it once. That's exactly why raising the price waits until after that happens.

The free offer hasn't produced a call yet

The previous version of this business emailed 589 coaches. People replied at a healthy rate — but nine real conversations produced zero calls. I was giving away a free diagnosis, and four of those nine read it, fixed the problem themselves, and left.

That's exactly why the ten names exist

You can't take ten real people and go do it yourself — the only way to get them is to be on the call with me. And the paid offer exists because a call that goes well needs something on the other side of it. Both are corrections to a failure I measured, not guesses.

One more thing worth knowing I built software that produces the free half. It researches each coach, finds the hook, checks the hook is real by fetching the source a second time, and refuses to put an email in the file if any number in it can't be traced to a real client result. It never sends anything — I upload the file myself. That's how I can make the ten-names promise at volume without it turning into spam.
